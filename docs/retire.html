Retirement math · GPIX + GPIQ

When can I retire?

This page answers one question: when do the dividends alone pay the monthly income you want? Each month you add money to two income funds, GPIX and GPIQ. They are covered-call funds: they sell options to earn extra income, and they give up part of a rise in the share price. You reinvest every dividend after 15% US tax. The page uses the funds' real 12-month dividend rates, updated with the other pages. Your job pay is not part of the math.
Crunching the numbers…

Method. The page runs a month-by-month simulation in your browser. Each month the funds pay dividends at their 12-month dividend rates, on what you hold at the start of the month. The tax comes off, and the rest is reinvested together with that month's savings. New money is split by your Mix. With the whole-share switch on, it buys whole shares, and money that cannot buy a whole share waits as cash. Share prices change by the path you choose. Your savings stay the same every month, and your goal grows with inflation. Your goal is reached in the first month when the dividends after tax are at least your goal. The money you already hold stays in its own fund, and the share prices come from the latest data run. Dividend rates come from data.json and data-gpiq.json.

Your numbers. The page saves your numbers only in your browser. Nothing is sent anywhere. Another device or browser keeps its own numbers.

What this is not. This page is not advice and not a promise. Dividend rates move with the option market, and covered-call funds give up part of a market rise to pay income. Read the tax box before you plan a big change in your life.
